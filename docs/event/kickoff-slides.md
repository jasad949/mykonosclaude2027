# Kickoff slides: what matters for building and presenting

A text summary of all 35 slides in [`docs/kickoff-slides.pdf`](../kickoff-slides.pdf) (the
organizers' copy has no speaker notes, and leaves out the credits QR and the prize slide). Read on
Nov 7, 2026. Names below are as printed on the slides.

## The big takeaways

1. **The 30-second video is the gate to the final.** "They watch every 30-second video, pick 10
   finalists and score each live demo." Only the 10 finalists present live, and only live demos are
   scored. A weak video means the demo is never seen. The submit slide calls it a "30-second
   Claude video... Made with Claude. Judges use it to pick the finalists."
2. **Four equal scoring areas:** Impact (a nonprofit or city would use it next month to help get
   homes built), Works (live demo on real data, sources shown; rough and working beats polished),
   Scale (a believable path to the next 100 projects), Innovation (a new way to see or solve the
   problem; bend the brief if it serves the goal).
3. **Scale is the framing of the whole event:** "Most solutions are small, one-off projects. Scale
   is missing. Is it attainable?"
4. **Each lane has one rule on its slide** (below). Breaking it is the easiest way to lose a judge.

## First steps (slide)

1. Open the hub. Register your team, then pick a room.
2. Pick a lane and **one real user**: who's stuck, and their before and after.
3. Ask a mentor, then build. Mentors and floaters rotate through every room.

A good team mixes a **builder**, an **idea person**, a **communicator** and an **organizer**. No
team, idea or coding experience needed.
