# Background: Habitat for Humanity of Orange County (public sources)

Pre-event research from public web pages, checked 2026-11-06. **It chooses no product.** Anything
here can be stale or wrong, so confirm it with Habitat OC staff before relying on it. Never
hardcode these figures into the product (see `EVENT_BRIEF.md`, "Financial figures").

Note that several unrelated affiliates share the name "Orange County" (in North Carolina, Florida and
Virginia). Ours is the California affiliate at **habitatoc.org**, 2200 Ritchey St, Santa Ana, CA 92705.

## How homeownership works there (from habitatoc.org)

1. **Orientation.** Selection "begins with our orientation". The credit-review form asks whether
   you attended one in the last 90 days.
2. **Qualifications.** There are three criteria:
   - **Need for improved housing.** Examples are overcrowded or unhealthy conditions, an unsafe
     neighborhood, paying more than 30% of gross monthly income for housing, or being unable to
     buy at market rate.
   - **Steady, verifiable income.** Applicants must be U.S. citizens or permanent residents who
     live or work in Orange County, and must be able to pay a long-term mortgage plus a 1–5% down
     payment and closing costs. A credit report is also reviewed and should be "in good standing".
   - **Willingness to partner.** This is a one-time "sweat equity" commitment of time. The site
     gives no hours figure.
3. **Partnership.** The applicant makes the equity contribution and the time commitment.
4. **Selection.** A Selection Committee recommends applicants to the Board of Directors, which
   approves.

**Income limits.** The site publishes a 2026 table of annual gross income by household size (1–8)
in three tiers: Very Low, Low and Moderate. For example, a household of 4 spans $55,850 to
$166,300. The source doesn't say how the tiers are used. Ask staff before using the table, and
if the demo needs it, load it as editable data with its source and date.

**Other programs.** They run Home Repair, a Veterans program, ReStore (in Santa Ana and Anaheim),
neighborhood revitalization and volunteering.

**Scale.** City of Santa Ana documents say the affiliate has completed and sold roughly 231–239
homes since 1988; sources differ. Current projects include 6 units at 1921 W. Washington Ave,
Santa Ana (groundbreaking July 2025), and HOME funds committed for 40 units on Alton Ave. An Oct
2026 gala was titled "Keys to 100", but the "100 homeownership opportunities" goal is still
**unconfirmed**, so ask staff.

## Pain points reported at other affiliates (to validate, not assume)

The evidence is thin and local, and none of it comes from Habitat OC.
- **Long process and drop-off.** One Houston study gives 6–12 months for admission and sweat
  equity, and 12–18 months end to end. Many applicants don't finish.
- **Paperwork burden.** One affiliate described the application, documentation and
  reimbursement processes as heavy, and said it relied on a volunteer to help applicants.
- **Sweat equity.** Applicants found the hours hard to fit around work and family (Habitat
  Portland report, 2025).
- **Application difficulty.** Some homeowners went through multiple denials before approval
  (Habitat Portland report).

## Questions this raises for Habitat OC staff

These add to the list in `EVENT_BRIEF.md`.
- How many people attend an orientation, apply, pass the credit review, and are selected? Where
  is the biggest drop-off?
- What do staff spend the most time on per applicant: document collection, answering the same
  questions, preparing committee packets, or tracking sweat equity?
- Which languages do applicants use? Is Spanish or Vietnamese support needed?
- What happens to applicants who are *not yet* ready (credit, down payment)? Is there a
  readiness path?
- What do the challenge's words "financially sustainable", meaning sustainable *after* purchase,
  mean to them: budgeting, maintenance costs, avoiding default?
